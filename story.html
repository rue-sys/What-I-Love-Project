MY STORY

More Than A Game.

Basketball became a part of my life without me really realizing how much it would mean to me.

01

HOW IT STARTED

It started as just a phase.

At first, basketball was simply something I enjoyed. I wasn't thinking about how far I could take it or whether I would become amazing at it.

My basketball journey started in the summer of 8th grade in W.K.M.A . I started because I loved trying new things, and I honestly thought basketball would just be another phase. I wanted to quit many times. But somehow, I never did.

Basketball became more than something I played; it became something that shaped me. The character it helped build in me is something I will carry on forever.

02

THE GROWTH

Effort changed my game.

Basketball taught me that being naturally good at something isn't everything. I wasn't the most talented player, but I kept playing and kept trying.

Seeing myself improve made me realize how much consistency matters. Every time I stepped onto the court, there was something new I could learn.

THE MOMENT

When I play, I don't overthink.

That might be one of the biggest reasons I love basketball. Usually, my mind can be somewhere else, thinking about too many things. But when I'm playing, I just focus on the game in front of me.

WHAT I LEARNED

Things I took off the court.

01

Adapt

Every player is different. Basketball taught me to adjust, pay attention and understand things from another player's point of view.

02

Compete

I discovered a competitive side of myself that I didn't always notice outside the court.

03

Teamwork

Basketball taught me that a win isn't only yours. It belongs to the whole team. I learned to trust others, adjust and see the game from another player's point of view.

04

Keep Going

Getting better takes time. I learned that showing up and putting in the work can change how far you go.

THE FUNNY PART

Basketball started as just a phase, but it's the one thing I never outgrew.

I don't play like I used to, but my love for basketball never really faded. It has changed with me, but it has never left.
